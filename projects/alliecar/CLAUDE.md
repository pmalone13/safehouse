## Pre-purchase checklist (before making an offer on any car)

1. Vehicle history report (Carfax/AutoCheck by VIN) — confirm clean title, no accidents, mileage consistency.
2. Independent mechanical inspection (or trusted local mechanic) — don't rely solely on the dealer's word.
3. Check maintenance records — evidence of regular oil changes, timing belt/CVT service if applicable.
4. Negotiate based on inspection findings + comparable listings above.
5. Once purchased: decide how/whether this should be reflected in the household `accounting/` books (e.g. Personal expense/gift to Allie) — not yet discussed with Paul.

## Paul's buying philosophy (from the strategy doc)

1. **Sizing the car to the need, not the budget ceiling.** Estimate ownership length x annual mileage (~12,000 mi/yr for Allie) + margin, to get a target remaining-life mileage. For Allie: expect to own 4-5 years, so target a car that can reasonably go **another 60,000 miles**.
2. **Usage-driven priorities.** Allie will do regular commuting plus occasional 500-1,000 mile trips, so comfort matters more than it would for Paul's own truck/hauling needs.
3. **A well-maintained older car beats a newer high-mileage one.** The core hunting principle: find the "oddball good car" — an example far better preserved/lower-mileage than typical for its year, not just the newest thing in budget.

## Initial search criteria (MUST HAVE)

- 2010 or newer
- 106,000 miles or less
- Target price $7,000 or less (a target, not a hard rule — e.g. a slightly-over-budget car within mileage limits can still be worth considering)
- Automatic transmission
- Four doors, sedan or crossover
- Good reliability history; reasonable maintenance/repair costs
- Clean title, no significant accident history
- Evidence of reasonable maintenance
- Exceptionally well-preserved for its age (clean interior, good paint/body, minimal rust/sun damage — "looks like somebody took care of it")

**Not important yet** (tie-breakers later): color, Bluetooth, stereo, wheels, navigation, sunroof, other convenience features.

## Process (in order)

1. **Pick a make/model** — Allie browses candidates matching the criteria above and picks 2-3 models she likes (not a specific car yet). Candidate list Paul suggested: Honda Civic, Honda Accord, Toyota Camry, Mazda3, Mazda6, Ford Fusion, Hyundai Elantra, Hyundai Sonata, Kia Forte, Kia Optima, selected small crossovers.
2. **Research generations** for the chosen model(s) — which model years share a platform/generation, which years/engines/transmissions are most reliable, which to avoid.
3. **Hunt for the oddball good car** — within the best generation, look for a specific example with unusually low mileage/great condition for its age, not just an average one.
4. **Search nationally** — expand beyond Virginia (VA -> Mid-Atlantic -> East Coast -> nationwide) if it surfaces a meaningfully better car; transport cost is roughly $500-1,000 and can be worth it.
5. **Before buying**: vehicle history report -> maintenance records -> condition check -> independent mechanical inspection -> negotiate -> buy.

plausible in a file listing; don't be fooled by that. The two text files

content. Everything under `projects/`
